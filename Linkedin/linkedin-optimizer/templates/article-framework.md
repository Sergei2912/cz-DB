# Article Framework

5 LinkedIn article templates by content pillar. Long-form format (1,500-2,000 words) for permanent profile features and Google SEO.

## Universal Article Structure

| Section | Word Count | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Title** | 6-12 words | SEO keyword + benefit/hook |
| **Featured Image** | — | 1200×627px, relevant, professional |
| **Introduction** | 150-200 | Hook → thesis → "what you'll learn" |
| **Section 1** | 300-400 | Context / Problem definition |
| **Section 2** | 400-500 | Core insight / Deep analysis |
| **Section 3** | 300-400 | Practical application / How-to |
| **Case Study** | 200-300 | Personal example + quantified results |
| **Conclusion** | 100-150 | 3 key takeaways + CTA |

**SEO Title Patterns:**
1. "X Things Every [Audience] Should Know About [Topic]"
2. "How [Action] Changed My [Outcome]: A [Specialty] Perspective"
3. "The Future of [Topic] in Israel: What [Audience] Need to Know"
4. "[Number]: The Metric That Defines [Aspect] in [Field]"
5. "From [Origin] to [Destination]: Lessons in [Topic]"

## Template 1: Clinical Cases Article

**Title pattern:** "X Lessons from [N]+ [Procedures]: What Every Endodontist Should Know"

### Introduction (150-200 words)
Hook with a surprising case outcome or statistic from your practice. State thesis: what this article teaches. Preview sections: "In this article, I'll share [N] lessons from my [X]+ procedures..."

### Section 1: The Challenge (300-400 words)
- Define the clinical challenge (e.g., calcified canals, retreatment complexity)
- Industry statistics on failure rates / complication rates
- Why this matters to patients and referring dentists
- Include 2-3 Medical Lexicon terms naturally

### Section 2: Deep Analysis (400-500 words)
- Technical breakdown of your approach
- Equipment and technique details (surgical microscope, NiTi files, CBCT)
- Evidence-based reasoning (cite ADA/AAE guidelines)
- Include data visualization suggestion (success rate chart, canal anatomy diagram)
- Weave in Key Metrics from Universita Hub

### Section 3: Practical Guide (300-400 words)
- Step-by-step approach for practitioners
- "Red flags" checklist — when to refer vs when to treat
- Equipment recommendations with honest cost-benefit
- Tips for case selection (which cases benefit most from microscope)

### Case Study (200-300 words)
- Specific case from your practice (anonymized)
- Before: patient presentation, initial diagnosis
- Action: your approach, tools used, decision points
- Result: quantified outcome (metrics from Universita Hub)
- Patient quote or satisfaction indicator (if available)

### Conclusion (100-150 words)
**3 Key Takeaways:**
1. [Clinical principle]
2. [Technology recommendation]
3. [Practice management insight]

**CTA:** "What's the most challenging case you've handled? I'd love to hear your approach in the comments."

**Tags:** 3-5 colleagues in endodontics who might reshare.

## Template 2: IDF Transition Article

**Title pattern:** "From IDF Captain to Civilian Practice: [N] Lessons in [Topic]"

### Introduction (150-200 words)
Open with a vivid military moment that connects to current practice. Thesis: how military service shapes better healthcare professionals. "After 8 years in the IDF Medical Corps, here's what I carry with me..."

### Section 1: Military Context (300-400 words)
- Describe IDF dental service structure (without classified details)
- Scale of operations: patients served, geographic coverage
- Unique challenges: field conditions, resource constraints, diverse populations
- Leadership responsibilities as Captain

### Section 2: Transferable Skills Deep Dive (400-500 words)
- **Triage mindset** → clinical decision-making under uncertainty
- **Resource optimization** → efficient practice management
- **Team leadership** → managing clinic staff, training junior dentists
- **Pressure performance** → handling emergencies, complex cases calmly
- Each skill with concrete military example → civilian parallel

### Section 3: The Transition Journey (300-400 words)
- Practical challenges: credential recognition, market entry, networking
- Cultural shifts: military hierarchy → private sector autonomy
- What helped: specific resources, mentors, strategies
- Advice for fellow veterans entering healthcare

### Case Study (200-300 words)
- Specific moment where IDF training directly helped in civilian practice
- Quantified result or patient outcome
- "My commander would say..." reference for authenticity

### Conclusion (100-150 words)
**3 Key Takeaways:**
1. [Leadership lesson]
2. [Practical transition advice]
3. [Value proposition for employers]

**CTA:** "Veterans in healthcare — what military skill do you use most? Non-veterans — what would you want to know about hiring IDF professionals?"

## Template 3: European Perspective Article

**Title pattern:** "Czech Republic to Israel: A Dentist's Guide to [Topic]"

### Introduction (150-200 words)
Open with a contrast moment: something that surprised you moving from European to Israeli practice. Thesis: international experience creates unique advantages.

### Section 1: European Training System (300-400 words)
- Czech dental education structure (Charles University context)
- Nostrifikace process — what it means and why it matters
- European standards vs Israeli standards (honest comparison)
- What European training excels at

### Section 2: Israeli Practice Landscape (400-500 words)
- How Israeli dental market differs (Kupot Holim system, private market)
- Pace, patient expectations, technology adoption
- Regulatory differences that affect daily practice
- The startup/MedTech ecosystem influence on dental innovation

### Section 3: Bridging Both Worlds (300-400 words)
- Specific advantages of dual-system experience
- How to leverage international credentials in Israel
- Advice for other immigrant professionals
- Cross-pollination: what Israel can learn from Europe and vice versa

### Case Study (200-300 words)
- Specific clinical situation where European training perspective helped
- Or: a cultural/professional moment that illustrates the value of both experiences

### Conclusion (100-150 words)
**3 Key Takeaways:**
1. [Credential insight]
2. [Cultural competence lesson]
3. [Career advantage for international professionals]

**CTA:** "Immigrant professionals — share your biggest surprise about working in Israel."

## Template 4: Tech in Dentistry Article

**Title pattern:** "Why [Technology] Will Define the Future of [Dental Specialty] in Israel"

### Introduction (150-200 words)
Open with a dramatic before/after enabled by technology. Thesis: specific technology is underadopted and offers massive clinical advantage.

### Section 1: The Problem (300-400 words)
- What traditional approach looks like (limitations)
- Failure rates, missed diagnoses, patient outcomes without technology
- Industry statistics from peer-reviewed sources
- Cost of NOT adopting technology (in outcomes, not just money)

### Section 2: The Technology Deep Dive (400-500 words)
- How it works (accessible explanation, not textbook)
- Your personal experience: hours logged, cases completed
- Data from your practice: detection rates, success rates (from Universita Hub)
- Include data visualization suggestion
- Address common objections (cost, learning curve, ROI timeline)

### Section 3: Implementation Guide (300-400 words)
- Getting started: equipment selection, training resources
- Learning curve expectations (honest timeline)
- ROI calculation for a typical Israeli clinic
- Tips for integrating into existing workflow

### Case Study (200-300 words)
- Specific case where technology made the difference
- Quantified outcome: what would have been missed without it
- Patient impact in human terms

### Conclusion (100-150 words)
**3 Key Takeaways:**
1. [Technology-specific insight]
2. [Adoption recommendation]
3. [Future prediction for Israeli market]

**CTA:** "Are you using [technology] in your practice? What's been your experience?"

## Template 5: Russian Patients Article

**Title pattern:** "Serving [N]M+ Russian-Speaking Israelis: What Healthcare Needs to Know"

### Introduction (150-200 words)
Open with a patient interaction moment that illustrates the language barrier impact. Thesis: linguistic and cultural competence is clinical infrastructure, not a nice-to-have.

### Section 1: The Demographics (300-400 words)
- Russian-speaking population in Israel: size, geographic distribution, demographics
- Healthcare access patterns and challenges
- Communication gaps in dental care specifically
- Impact on treatment compliance and outcomes

### Section 2: Cultural Competence in Practice (400-500 words)
- Language barriers in clinical communication
- Cultural attitudes toward dental care (prevention, pain management, authority)
- Trust-building with Russian-speaking patients
- Specific communication strategies that work
- Include Medical Lexicon Hebrew-Russian terminology where relevant

### Section 3: For Clinic Owners (300-400 words)
- Business case for multilingual staff
- Geographic targeting (cities with high Russian-speaking population)
- Marketing to Russian-speaking communities (channels, messaging)
- Hiring bilingual professionals: what to look for

### Case Study (200-300 words)
- Patient interaction where language changed the outcome
- Compliance improvement with native-language explanation
- Community impact: word-of-mouth in Russian-speaking networks

### Conclusion (100-150 words)
**3 Key Takeaways:**
1. [Clinical communication insight]
2. [Business opportunity for clinics]
3. [Cultural competence recommendation]

**CTA:** "Healthcare professionals — how do you handle multilingual patient care? Share your strategies."
